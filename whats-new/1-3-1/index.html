Locus 1.3.1

Smoother Places, clearer Real Objects.

Places is smoother to browse, Real Objects tells you where each model comes from and whether it works on your visionOS, and water in supported Views looks more natural. Library updates and editing the bar are easier, too.

Available now for Apple Vision Pro

About this update

Locus 1.3.1 is a smaller update to Locus 1.3.0, available on the App Store. In-app What’s New still describes Locus 1.3. New Library content may arrive separately. Check your installed version in Settings → Help & About → About Locus.

Places is smoother to browse.

Places now draws its cards one at a time, starting at the top left, with placeholders until each card is ready, so the page stays responsive while it fills in. Names and descriptions of Places in your language are also more complete.

Real Objects shows where a model comes from.

A Real Object’s detail now shows the source facts we have confirmed for it, such as how it was made, its author, its license and links to its source. Facts we don’t have are left out rather than guessed.

Each model also shows the visionOS version it needs before you get it. A reference object trained with Xcode 27 requires visionOS 27, and on visionOS 26 Locus now says so instead of offering a model that can’t load. If a model fails to load, Locus tells you which one and what to do, rather than showing it as active. Straw options now appear only for cups that come with a separate straw version.

Making your own object? The Real Objects modeling guide walks through it.

More natural water.

In supported Views, water reflections, the transition to the shore and the detail you see under the surface are refined. These are capabilities a View can include, so you will see them only where a View has been updated to use them.

More reliable Library updates.

Library updates now recover more reliably when something interrupts them, including updates to the Place you are in.

Easier Quick Controls and bar editing.

In Settings → Workspace, Edit Quick Controls and Customize Bar now open as full pages, so you see about ten rows at once. Enabled controls are listed first, and you can drag a switched-off control up among them to turn it on.
