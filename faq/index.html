FAQ

Useful answers, without the small print.

How Locus works with Apple Vision Pro, websites, custom places, and continued development.

Using Locus

What is the difference between a View and a Room?

A View is the 360° scenery around a place. A Room is its 3D architecture: the floor, walls, windows, desk position, openings, and other geometry you can move within. Locus combines them into an experience.

What is the difference between Virtual Space and Room Portal?

Virtual Space places you inside an authored Room and keeps the real desk available for work. Room Portal keeps your real room present and replaces one chosen wall with a View. Room Portal does not turn every wall or the ceiling into a portal.

How do I use Locus with Mac Virtual Display?

Mac Virtual Display normally does not remain available inside immersive apps. Apple provides a developer setting that can allow it: pair Apple Vision Pro with Xcode, enable Developer Mode, restart the headset, then enable Settings → Developer → Allow Mac Virtual Display. Connect to your Mac from Control Center before entering a Locus place.

visionOS—not Locus—creates and controls the Mac window. Locus works normally without Developer Mode or Mac Virtual Display. See the complete Mac Virtual Display guide for requirements and limitations.

Why can passkey sign-in fail in the Locus browser?

Locus uses Apple WebKit for websites. Standard passkey requests are handed to WebKit and visionOS; Locus never reads or stores your passwords or passkeys. Compatibility can still differ from Safari: some websites only enable passkeys for browsers they recognize, and Apple Passwords or third-party credential-provider handoff has not yet been verified on a physical Vision Pro for every site.

If a site offers another sign-in method, use that method for now. When reporting a failure, include the website, visionOS version, and the step that failed—never include a credential or recovery code.

Why does a download open outside Locus?

The current Locus browser does not save downloaded files. When a download has a safe public URL, Locus can offer it to the system browser or another app; you may need to sign in or start the download again there. Your current Locus environment and tabs remain available when you return.

Custom Views and Rooms

What custom files can I import?

Import a View as one complete 2:1 equirectangular JPEG, PNG, or HEIC image from Photos, Files, or a direct public HTTPS URL. Import a Room from Files or a direct public HTTPS URL as a .locusplace or ordinary .zip archive containing at least one Room.

Locus does not directly import a .blend, GLB, OBJ, raw catalog/ folder, arbitrary directory, standalone Experience, or View-only .locusplace. See Create your own Place for the supported package layout.

What does a skybox or View image need?

Use one complete 360° equirectangular image in JPEG, PNG, or HEIC format. Its displayed width must be exactly twice its height—for example, 12,288 × 6,144, which is recommended but not required. Partial panoramas, cube-face sets, and multi-frame images are not supported.

Keep the horizon level. Put the direction you want to face first at the horizontal center of the image; the 360° seam belongs at the far left and right edges. Inspect that seam at full size for jumps in architecture, lighting, sky, or texture before importing. Locus normalizes the image and prepares its preview and Room lighting locally.

How can I make my own house or Room?

Model the architecture in Blender at real-world meter scale with +Y up, self-contained materials, and sensible geometry. Export a USDZ, then describe its seated origin, safe head volume, destination openings, collision behavior, and quality measurements in a Locus Room package. Run the public packer and validator before choosing Import a Room.

You can build it by hand, or ask an AI agent connected to Blender through Blender MCP to create and iterate on the house with you. The agent can speed up modeling, material work, checks, and export, but it does not replace your review of scale, seating, openings, comfort, or performance—and Locus still requires the same validated Room package.

Start with the Room authoring guide and examples.

Why does importing more of my own content cost money?

Everyone can use Locus's built-in library, and the included allowance lets you keep one custom View and one custom Room. A larger personal library needs either the one-time More Imports unlock or an active Supporter subscription.

The charge supports the ongoing work behind safe package validation, rendering, library management, visionOS compatibility, support, and continued Locus development.

What is the Supporter subscription for?

Supporter enables unlimited custom imports while the subscription is active, directly supports continued Locus development, and provides access to Supporter place packs as they are released. There is no promised pack schedule.

Installed content is not deleted when a subscription ends. New imports above the included allowance and re-downloading a removed Supporter pack require active access. If you only want unlimited imports of your own Views and Rooms, the one-time More Imports option is the permanent alternative. Current prices and purchase terms are shown inside Locus by the App Store.

Privacy and support

Where do my browsing and imported-place data go?

Website cookies and site data may be stored in Locus's WebKit profile on your device; Locus provides controls to clear history and website data. Imported Place packages are validated and stored in the app's local library. The current app does not upload room meshes or camera images to Locus servers. Read the privacy notice for the complete boundary.

What should I include in a support report?

Include the Locus version, build number and short commit from Settings → About Locus, your visionOS version, and the smallest safe steps that reproduce the problem. Do not put room images, credentials, recovery codes, receipts, order details, account information, notifications, or device identifiers in a public issue. Use Support to choose a public or private route.
