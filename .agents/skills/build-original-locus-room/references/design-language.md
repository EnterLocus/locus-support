# Locus Room design language

This is an aesthetic and product brief, not a modeling tutorial. Use it to make
an original Room feel coherent beside the public Locus examples without
copying their floor plans or recognizable details.

## Character

Aim for a quiet contemporary pavilion: clear planar structure, disciplined
proportions, warm natural surfaces, restrained dark frames, and one memorable
spatial idea. Prefer a few legible masses over many decorative objects. The
Room should feel calm enough for sustained seated use and specific enough to
have an identity in the catalog.

## Spatial priorities

- Design outward from the seated eye point and intended View.
- Give each seat a clear activity, focal direction, and comfortable enclosure.
- Use structure, a rear service spine, a ceiling plane, or furniture to balance
  broad glazing with a sense of shelter.
- Make openings generous and intentional. A Room with three glazed sides
  should read as a pavilion, not as missing walls.
- Keep circulation and floor edges visually unambiguous from the seat.
- Use one strong silhouette or sectional move rather than unrelated accents.

## Material and color discipline

Use a small palette such as pale mineral surfaces, warm timber, charcoal metal,
clear glass, and one muted accent. Keep roughness and scale plausible. Glass
needs a readable edge or frame but should not be made visible by excessive
mirror-like reflections. Avoid baked shadows or highlights that contradict a
visitor-controlled lamp.

For an independently redistributable original, prefer authored procedural
materials or textures whose redistribution rights are documented. Record every
embedded third-party component even when attribution is optional.

## Lighting behavior

Treat the luminaire as architecture. Its fixture body should be recognizable
and visibly on whenever its Locus control is enabled, from near and far. Use a
bounded direct proxy for the task pool. If broad bounce is authored, keep it
soft and low-frequency so Overall Room EV can scale room response without
creating a second fixture-shaped glow.

For one-seat rooms, begin with one logical light group and one direct source.
Add more only when the physical design genuinely contains separately
controllable emitters. Verify that the fixture, direct pool, and indirect layer
agree spatially from the seated view and the catalog preview.

## Catalog readiness

The thumbnail should communicate the Room's defining idea at a glance: its
silhouette, the primary seat or surface, the View opening, and the lighting
character. Avoid a composition dominated by empty floor, clipped structure, or
an angle no visitor can experience after entry.
